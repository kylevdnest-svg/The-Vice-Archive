# Game vision — The Vice Archive

**As of:** 2026-11-06  
**Status:** Approved high-level concept; NOT an implemented game.

## Product identity

- **Game:** The Vice Archive (working title approved for planning, not trademark-cleared).
- **Studio/persona:** SableInk, solo developer.
- **Genre:** Small, finite, single-player **adult management / character game**, intended for commercial Windows release, ideally Steam subject to platform rules.
- **Setting:** A modern, exclusive private fantasy studio in a renovated theatre. Luxurious noir/gothic flavour, not another cyberpunk city simulation.
- **Audience:** Primarily heterosexual male players, with some optional appeal for female and gay players. Do not promise equal content across audiences without production approval.
- **Player:** Limited character/identity choices; occasional personal participation, not a second giant gameplay system.
- **Content:** Adult performers and adult clients; primarily authored scenes, with limited interactivity where it justifies the workload. Characters' boundaries and consent are part of the premise.
- **Tone:** Not finally decided. The author answered "whatever sells"; validate commercial appeal instead of treating earlier tone suggestions as final.

## The distinguishing experience

**Fantasy creation and matching** is the unique mechanic and should receive the most attention.

Proposed core loop:
1. Receive a client's fantasy/request and constraints.
2. Choose an appropriate adult performer.
3. Choose limited experience parameters (room/theme/props or preparation).
4. Resolve appointment using simple, controlled rules.
5. Receive client/performer outcome, payment and authored adult reward/progression.
6. Reinvest in limited upgrades, character stories and the next challenge.

Player should understand *why* their choice worked, with minimal busywork. The management and relationships support the fantasy-creation mechanic rather than overshadowing it.

## Experience priorities

1. Appealing adult content.
2. Gameplay that makes unlocking adult content worthwhile.
3. Clear progress from a struggling establishment to a successful destination, with a defined campaign ending.
4. Recognisable cast, light relationship consequences, appealing presentation.
5. Readable, scalable UI and quick interaction. No intimidating dashboard.

## Explicit constraints

- **No sandbox-first or never-ending simulation.** Finite campaign with goals and ending.
- Simple stats/behaviour; **VERY controlled variation**, not unbounded randomness.
- Small cast, one venue, and constrained combinations. Avoid combinatorial explosion.
- Do not introduce procedural cities, large psychological systems, excessive economy, multiplayer or servers.
- No live/generated AI required for runtime gameplay; offline, local saves.
- No announced release or price without approval.
- Author wants a repeatable commercial game-development process; this game should be manageable and complete.

## Originality and reference

The Coffin of Andy and Leyley was selected as a **visual reference for simplified, expressive indie 2D illustration**, not as a character, plot or asset source. The Vice Archive must have its own art direction: mature adult characters, bold readable shapes, expressive eyes, clean linework, restrained shading, dark theatrical palettes, recognisable character silhouettes.

## Success criteria (design, not validated)

A tester should leave saying something close to: **"The adult content is fantastic, and the gameplay makes unlocking it worthwhile."**

No validation yet that the fantasy-matching mechanic is fun or that the art pipeline can meet commercial quality.
